# Detection coverage — retrospective, April 2026

**Written:** 2026-04-23, at the close of the Phase 3 content lift. **Extended:** 2026-04-23 with the Phase 4 entity-propagation section, the Phase 3.5 parked-investigation section, and the Phase 5 label-adjacent-detection section. **Extended:** 2026-04-24 with the Phase 6 structured-outputs parked-investigation section, closing out the April detection-coverage plan.
**Sibling documents:** `docs/detection-coverage-plan-2026-04.md` (forward-looking plan, complete as far as executable work goes — Phases 3.5 and 6 parked with diagnosed mechanisms) and `docs/viewer-rework-plan-2026-04.md` (parallel work-stream, Phases 1 and 2 shipped, Phase 3 paused).
**Purpose:** one document that lets a future reader — Eugene, a fresh Cowork conversation, or a developer joining later — understand what the April 2026 detection-quality work tried, what worked, what didn't, and the conventions that now govern bench quality and detection changes.

## 1. The starting point

On 20 April 2026, the B1 HR investigation fixture — a four-page DOCX about a workplace bullying complaint — was the mirror the detection pipeline could not quite recognise itself in. The narrow set of things that worked revealed, by contrast, the breadth of what didn't.

What worked, pre-plan: the regex patterns for NZ identifiers (IRD, driver licence, email, most phone formats, addresses) caught the deterministic data in B1's personal-details panel. Azure DI extracted the document's layout and Azure OpenAI `gpt-4o` with the in-production prompt flagged many obvious personal names — the complainant Melissa Ferguson and the respondent David Kellogg by full name, and their dates of birth.

What didn't work fell into several categories. The GP landline `(06) 759 2217` was missed because the phone regex at `lib/pipeline/patterns.ts:115` could not cross the `)` character between area code and number. The respondent's NZ passport `LA429183` was missed because `nz-passport` was default-off in `DEFAULT_DETECTION_TOGGLES`. The complainant's driver licence `EA123456` and employee numbers `ADC-2284` / `ADC-0917` sat inside labelled table rows where neither regex nor AI connected label to value. Surname-only references — the dozens of bare `Ferguson` and `Kellogg` instances scattered through the narrative body — were absent entirely from the output, even though the full-name anchors had been flagged.

The richer misses lived in contextual content. B1's document contains a section literally headed "Findings — free and frank" and a separate subsection citing privileged legal advice from external counsel Ben Mahuika at Holroyd Partners, including a settlement range of `$55,000 — $110,000`. Neither the candid investigator commentary nor the privileged settlement range surfaced reliably as dedicated `free-frank` or `legal-privilege` detections — the AI flagged adjacent text irregularly, sometimes as `personal-name`, sometimes not at all. Dr Sarah Liang, the complainant's GP named in the medical section, was missed in 0 of 3 gpt-4o runs. Witness names like Jonathan Briggs, Priya Sharma, and Mere Rauhihi — identities that exist in a bullying grievance context and therefore sit under s7(2)(f)(ii) `harassment-risk` protection — were missed uniformly by both models tested. The medical diagnosis prose ("adjustment disorder with mixed anxiety and depressed mood") and its ICD-10 code `F43.23` were missed.

The scope of what was missed mattered more than the volume. These were not edge cases in an otherwise solid pipeline; they were the content categories that real LGOIMA release decisions turn on — free-and-frank internal advice (s7(2)(f)(i)), harassment-risk witness identity (s7(2)(f)(ii)), privileged legal content (s7(2)(g)), third-party professional identity (s7(2)(a)), commercial-in-confidence content on future C1 tender fixtures (s7(2)(b)), and public-infrastructure protective measures (s7(2)(d)). If the pipeline could not find these in a small, hand-verified test fixture, it was not ready to ask reviewers to trust it at scale. That was the frame for the April work.

## 2. What we tried, in order

The plan at `docs/detection-coverage-plan-2026-04.md` split the work into seven phases. Some ran in parallel, some produced surprises that spawned sub-phases. The narrative below follows the actual order things landed, not the plan's clean sequence.

### Phase 0 — model-choice spike

Before committing to four to six weeks of prompt rework on `gpt-4o`, the plan called for a head-to-head spike against `o4-mini` on Azure `eastus`. The hypothesis: a more capable reasoning model might render the prompt rework unnecessary.

The spike ran three times per condition against B1 and produced a clear answer: stay on `gpt-4o`. On governance-pathway content, `gpt-4o` matched or beat `o4-mini` (legal-privilege 3/3 vs 3/3 on Ben Mahuika flags, free-and-frank 3/3 vs 2/3). `o4-mini` did win on two specific things — third-party professional detection (Dr Sarah Liang 2/3 vs 0/3; Ben Mahuika as dedicated `personal-name` 1/3 vs 0/3) and parenthesised-area-code phone (3/3 vs 1/3) — but the phone win was moot because a regex fix would catch the same thing deterministically, and the third-party-professional gain was traceable to `o4-mini` being less rigid about the prompt's existing "don't flag council officials" instruction. The fix was in the prompt, not the model.

`o4-mini` was also roughly 5.4× slower per batch (61 seconds median vs 11 seconds), which ruled it out for synchronous user-facing detection regardless of any quality wins. The spike artefacts sit at `docs/spike-model-comparison-2026-04-20/` for anyone wanting to re-verify the decision later.

One thing the spike exposed accidentally turned out to matter more than the spike itself, but the full story of that belongs in Phase 1.5. The spike harness called `extractText(buf, "DOCX")` directly, which routed to `extractFromDocx` — a mammoth-based path that returns a single page regardless of the underlying DOCX structure. It bypassed the production `buildCanonicalPdf` + DI-on-canonical path entirely. That produced a one-page extraction from B1, which led to a brief but wrong hypothesis that the production pipeline was collapsing long documents. That hypothesis is what Phase 1.5 eventually resolved. The spike harness itself is now tracked as issue #14 — future spike and bench harnesses that test the production DOCX flow must route through `buildCanonicalPdf` first.

### Phase 1 — hotfixes

Phase 1 bundled four small, correctness-oriented changes that did not need benchmark infrastructure to validate. It shipped in two deploys.

**cr16** was the landline regex fix. The existing regex at `lib/pipeline/patterns.ts:115` was rewritten to handle parenthesised area codes, and eleven new regression test cases — each one a format that appears in real council correspondence, `(06) 759 2217`, `+64 (6) 759 2217`, `06-759-2217`, and so on — were added. Verified on the live `veil.datasing.nz` instance after deploy; the GP landline on B1 now flags.

**cr17** bundled three changes: `nz-passport` and `vehicle-reg` flipped to default-enabled in `DEFAULT_DETECTION_TOGGLES`; a split of `AZURE_OPENAI_DEPLOYMENT_DETECTION` and `AZURE_OPENAI_DEPLOYMENT_CLASSIFICATION` env vars to future-proof any model-split decision without a code change; and a single-batch guard so documents with `preparedPages.length ≤ AI_DETECT_SINGLE_BATCH_MAX_PAGES` (default 6) run as a single AI call rather than splitting at `BATCH_SIZE=3`. Five new unit tests, all green. Deployed and verified.

Phase 1 was cheap — roughly two engineering days — and produced durable wins with no downstream obligations. It deliberately did not try to benchmark the lift; Phase 2 was already in motion to provide proper measurement. Its main function, in retrospect, was clearing out the deterministic-detection gaps so that benchmark variance later on could be attributed to AI non-determinism rather than tangled-up regex holes.

### Phase 1.5 — extraction investigation (resolved as a spike-harness bug, not a production bug)

Because the Phase 0 spike extracted B1 to a single canonical page, and the plan's Phase 4 entity-propagation design assumed cross-batch entity continuity on multi-page documents, Phase 1.5 was inserted to establish whether the production pipeline actually collapsed DOCX to single pages — which would have made Phase 4 speculative at best.

The investigation is documented at `docs/phase-1-5-extraction-findings.md`. In short: the production pipeline was fine. The issue was entirely in the spike harness. Production routes through `buildCanonicalPdf` first — LibreOffice renders the DOCX to a multi-page canonical PDF, Azure DI `prebuilt-read` then extracts word-level polygons against that PDF, and the result respects page breaks. cr17 production logs confirmed B1 extracts to four canonical pages (5,596 characters) in production, matching the visual rendering.

The secondary finding — that LibreOffice-nogui's `javaldx` helper prints a cosmetic warning at conversion time that installing `default-jre` does not suppress — was documented but not acted on, because conversion exits 0 and produces correct multi-page output regardless. Other DOCX fixtures (`04_main_case_file_long`, `05_internal_briefing_and_recommendation`, `06_supporting_statements_and_appendices`) all produced 8-page canonical PDFs in the Docker environment, confirming B1's short length was a fixture property rather than a pipeline-level page collapse.

Phase 1.5 cost about half a day and produced no code changes. Its value was the negative finding: Phase 4's entity-propagation premise was confirmed valid, and the spike-harness artefact had not corrupted any production-deployed behaviour. Issue #14 captures the guard-rail follow-up: spike and bench harnesses must route through the production canonical-PDF path or explicitly opt out with a comment.

### Phase 1.75 — the bbox-stripping fix for long narrative AI detections

Phase 1.75 was not in the original plan. It emerged during Phase 2 bench harness work, specifically from the first live baseline run against the then-newly-authored five-fixture corpus.

The baseline capture showed something structurally wrong. On the C1 commercial-tender fixture, across three runs, `gpt-4o` produced nine detections per run — nine detections that never arrived at the `Detection` table. On the A governance-memo fixture, the pattern was softer but the same shape: six, seven, and three AI detections produced respectively per run, of which only two survived to storage each time. B3, a ten-page inquiry fixture with deliberate long-narrative health-safety protective-measure content, stored zero of the detections the AI produced for that content. The bench scored every one of those fixtures as a catastrophe on their target pathways — commercial at 0.000 F1, A at 0.095 F1, governance pathway at 0.044 F1 overall.

Because the bench showed both the "AI produced" and the "Detections stored" counts in its logs, the shape of the problem was obvious quickly: something between the AI's response and the Prisma insert was silently dropping long-text detections. The investigation took about a day and is written up at `docs/phase-1-75-ai-detection-stripping-findings.md`. The cause: `calculateBBoxAll` at `lib/pipeline/bbox.ts:24` returned an empty array when detection text exceeded 80 characters, and the enrichment loop in `process.ts:660` iterated the returned array to produce per-detection `enrichedDetections` entries — empty array meant zero iterations meant zero stored.

The fault was small in code and significant in implication. The CLAUDE.md documentation at the time described the behaviour as: "long AI narrative summaries short-circuit to zero bbox and fall through to Tier 2 text-search." That was aspirational — the first half existed (the 80-char short-circuit), the second half did not (there was no zero-bbox placeholder emitted, no fall-through, just a silent drop). The fix added the zero-bbox placeholder at the enrichment site so that long-narrative detections reached storage and the review UI. Approximately fifteen lines of code. Deployed as cr18 within a day of merge.

The downstream implication was not resolved in the fix. Tier 2 auto-redaction at `redact-pdf.ts:360` still enforces its own 80-character cap, so long-narrative detections now reach the review UI but depend on the manual-detection flow for actual redaction. That gap is issue #20, priority medium, deliberately not folded into the Phase 1.75 fix because separating "make detections visible" from "make detections auto-redactable" kept the fix reviewable and the production risk low. A reviewer can accept a long-narrative detection today and apply it manually; a follow-up PR will close the loop properly.

### Phase 2 — benchmark harness

Phase 2 ran in three tranches across 20–21 April and produced the measurement infrastructure the rest of the plan depended on.

Tranche 1 shipped the scoring library (`lib/bench/scoring.ts`), pathway map (`lib/bench/pathways.ts`), and reusable runner (`lib/bench/bench-runner.ts`) with a standalone CLI. The scoring semantics were deliberately tight from the start: type agreement required (an actual `personal-name` detection cannot satisfy an expected `confidential` entry, even with identical text), exact-or-substring text matching under case-insensitive whitespace normalisation, optional page-number agreement, and first-match-wins (each actual detection satisfies at most one expected entry; surplus actuals count as false positives). Per-pathway aggregates — personal, commercial, governance, enforcement — were computed alongside the overall score from the outset, because a prompt change that lifts one pathway while silently degrading another is exactly the failure mode the bench needed to catch.

Tranche 2 split into four pieces. Tranche 2a-i authored the B1/B2/A/C1 `.expected.json` ground truths: Eugene wrote B1 from scratch as the incident anchor for his own judgement, and Claude drafted B2, A, and C1 against local text-verification checks with Eugene reviewing. The `A_Council_Memo_Candid_Advice.docx` and `C1_Tender_Evaluation_Commercial.docx` fixtures were authored synthetically — NPC-style council content designed to exercise governance and commercial pathways respectively — and generated byte-reproducibly via `scripts/generate-bench-fixtures.ts` so the sha256 of each fixture matches exactly across generator re-runs. Tranche 2a-ii added B3, a ten-page inquiry-report fixture deliberately long enough to force cross-batch detection in the production AI pipeline. Tranche 2b wired it all to production by implementing `lib/bench/pipeline-invoker.ts` (which seeds a dedicated `Case` record per bench run to isolate from prod seed data, uploads the fixture, invokes `processDocument`, queries detections, and cleans up in a finally block) and the suite runner (`scripts/bench/bench-suite.ts`) that orchestrates N runs per fixture with detection union across runs. Tranche 3 landed the CI regression guard as a GitHub Actions workflow that runs the bench on every PR touching the detection pipeline or the bench infrastructure itself, posts a PR comment with per-fixture and per-pathway delta tables, and fails CI on regression beyond threshold.

The first live baseline captured on 21 April against the then-current pipeline (pre-Phase-1.75-fix for the first four-fixture capture; post-fix for the five-fixture one) landed at `docs/bench-baselines/baseline-2026-04-21-5fixtures/`. Suite F1 0.481. Per-pathway: personal 0.615, commercial 0.467, governance **0.282**, enforcement 0.182. This became the first anchor.

Phase 2 was the single most valuable piece of infrastructure built in April. Every subsequent decision — threshold tuning, the N=10 rebaseline, the Phase 3 PR B judgment-zone call — was grounded in numbers this harness produced, not in impressions of detection quality.

### Phase 3 PR A — cache-friendly prompt restructure

Phase 3 split deliberately into two PRs. PR A was the structural housekeeping, PR B the content rework. Shipping them separately meant reviewers could approve the behaviour-neutral refactor without also having to assess the substantive prompt changes, and the cache benefit landed before PR B's iteration rounds began.

The caching point is worth explaining, because the change is easy to mistake for cosmetic. Azure OpenAI supports prompt caching on GPT-4o-series models for stable system-prompt prefixes of at least 1,024 tokens within a five-minute TTL, billing cached input tokens at roughly half the normal rate. Before PR A, `buildSystemPrompt()` prepended `buildClassificationContext()` — per-document content including document type, likely grounds, and classification flags — at the start of the system message. That made the prefix per-document-unique. Cache hit rate zero. PR A moved the classification block to the tail of the system message, behind the stable type descriptions, grounds reference, worked examples, "Important context" block, and JSON output spec. The long stable prefix became cacheable; the second and third batches of any multi-batch document now land fully cached within the TTL window.

Beyond the cache benefit, PR A extended the `personal-name` type description with one sentence about labelled values in tables — groundwork for PR B's Example 12 — and added two unit tests asserting the new system-prompt ordering. Behaviour was intended to be unchanged and was verified so: directional B2 bench F1 moved from 0.581 to 0.588 (+0.007, inside noise). Merged as PR #26 with no regression signal on the bench.

### Issue #27 — the N=10 rebaseline

PR A's CI run was the first time the CI regression guard fired on the full five-fixture suite against the single-run canonical. It produced an interesting failure. B2's F1 read 0.571 against a canonical of 0.722 — a 15.1 percentage point drop. Two further CI re-runs against identical code produced 0.625 and 0.703. The pipeline had not changed; B2's F1 was moving across a wide distribution because of AI non-determinism, and the canonical happened to sit at the top of that distribution.

The CI threshold, which began at 8pp per-fixture on the initial assumption that single-run variance was modest, was raised twice: first to 12pp, then to 16pp, each time in response to a false-positive trip on identical code. At 16pp the gate still held but was not meaningfully distinguishing real regressions from run-to-run noise — a 16pp F1 drop on a 20-entry fixture can be produced by swapping two true-positive detections for two false-positive ones, which is ordinary AI non-determinism on a tight corpus.

Issue #27 captured the proper fix: re-capture the canonical from N=10 independent suite samples, take the per-fixture median F1 as the reference, and set the CI threshold based on observed low-side deviation from the median rather than on the top-of-range value of a single-run anchor.

Implementation: a new capture script at `scripts/bench/bench-canonical-capture.ts` that runs the suite N=10 times (10 samples × 3 unioned runs × 5 fixtures = 150 pipeline invocations, roughly $12–15 of Azure spend, ~50 minutes wall time). Each sample's per-fixture `FixtureScore` is preserved in a `samples/` audit trail, and the aggregate takes the median F1, median TP, FP, FN per fixture plus the per-pathway medians. Idempotent — an interrupted capture resumes from where it left off.

The first N=10 capture landed at `docs/bench-baselines/baseline-2026-04-23-median-N10/`. Per-fixture F1 distributions showed B1 stddev 1.2pp, B2 6.1pp, A 4.3pp, C1 5.5pp, B3 2.9pp. The variance range is wide across fixtures but the low-side deviations from each fixture's median — the number the CI threshold must actually absorb for false-positive avoidance — were B1 1.2pp, B2 7.0pp (worst), A 5.4pp, C1 3.4pp, B3 4.3pp. The threshold was accordingly dropped back to **12pp** with roughly 5pp of headroom over the worst observed low-side noise. Suite aggregate threshold stayed at 5pp because per-fixture noise averages across fixtures.

The governance pathway anchor moved from 0.282 (single-run, lucky-low reading) to **0.337** (median of ten three-run samples, the honest reading). That re-anchored every subsequent Phase 3 PR B target. A lift to ~0.40 is +6.3pp, not the +11.8pp the single-run anchor implied. Merged as PR #28, closing issue #27.

### Phase 3 PR B — governance-pathway content lift

PR B was the content rework PR A had set up for. Four deliverables:

First, the council-official carve-out at `lib/pipeline/ai-detect.ts` was reworded to distinguish three cases that the previous single-sentence rule conflated. Council's own elected officials and senior managers acting on policy — not flagged. Third-party professional service providers named in the document body (external counsel, GPs, specialist contractors, mental-health providers, auditors, consultants) — flagged as `personal-name` even when operating professionally. Investigators and HR staff acting in a grievance or disciplinary context — flagged as `harassment-risk` when their identity co-occurs with grievance-specific detail. This was the change the Phase 0 spike had predicted would close the Dr Sarah Liang / Ben Mahuika miss class on `gpt-4o`.

Second, eleven new worked examples (9–19) went in after Example 8. Examples 9–18 covered HR candid commentary, legal-privileged settlement ranges, witness identity in grievance, labelled DOB in a table, labelled employee number, labelled driver licence, third-party professional, commercial third-party prejudice, public health-safety protective measure, and obligation-of-confidence whistleblower. Example 19, added under the v3.2 plan amendment, covered medical diagnosis in prose as `personal-name` under s7(2)(a) — the pattern that B1's `adjustment disorder with mixed anxiety and depressed mood` had exposed.

Third, a new structural-heuristics block was inserted between the `DETECTION GUIDANCE BY GROUND` section and the worked examples. Section-header-aware rules for `(free and frank)` / `without prejudice` / `in confidence` sections, with an explicit split between s7(2)(f)(i) advice-candour protection and s7(2)(f)(ii) witness-identity protection within those sections. Commercial vocabulary triggers, public-health-safety triggers, tikanga-cultural literal-term triggers, an investigation-document witness-name rule, and the labelled-value rule joined in the same block.

Fourth, `test-fixtures/bench/B1.expected.json` had Sarah Mitchell's type retuned from `personal-name` to `harassment-risk` (and pathway from `personal` to `governance`). The reworded carve-out plus Example 11 semantically placed investigator-in-grievance identity under s7(2)(f)(ii), so leaving the ground-truth at `personal-name` would have caused the scorer's type-agreement requirement to register every successful Mitchell detection as a simultaneous false negative on the `personal-name` entry and a false positive on the `harassment-risk` side.

The iteration during PR B was a small but instructive moment. A single-run directional bench after the initial prompt edits showed B3's false-positive count jumping from a canonical median of 26.5 to 39. Inspection of the structural-heuristics block revealed the cause: the initial wording told the model to "treat every substantive sentence in that section as a strong candidate for detection." On B3's lengthy "Findings — free and frank" section, that was firing on procedural content — attendance lists, interview dates, document citations, case numbers — not on the candid-opinion content the rule was meant to protect. A targeted tightening went in: "Do NOT flag factual procedural content inside these sections... The heuristic protects candour, not procedure." Re-run the directional bench, B3 came back to noise-band, governance direction recovered.

The N=10 post-PR-B canonical landed with governance pathway at **0.345** — above the 0.337 anchor but below the +2pp "clear merge" bar of 0.357. This put the result squarely in the judgment zone. Other pathways moved cleanly: commercial +6.5pp (Examples 16 and 18 firing on C1 and on whistleblower-style content), personal +1.7pp (third-party professionals releasing), B1 +6.6pp (Mitchell retune plus carve-out), C1 +5.4pp (commercial example firing on tender content). Suite aggregate +0.5pp. B2 dropped 1.3pp on the median, well under the 5pp watch threshold, and its standard deviation tightened meaningfully — from 6.1pp to 2.0pp — producing more consistent output even as the headline number slipped slightly.

The pragmatic call was to merge PR B in the judgment zone rather than iterate the prompt further. The reasoning: the commercial and personal lifts were clean and attributable to specific changes; governance is the hardest pathway to move with prompt rules alone because its content lives in free-frank and harassment-risk territory where the right detection requires propagating identity across a whole document (Phase 4's territory) rather than labelling one sentence better; and further iteration on governance prompts risked eroding the clear gains elsewhere. Phase 4 entity propagation inherits 0.345 as its new governance starting point. PR #29, merged on 23 April, closed the Phase 3 scope.

### Phase 4 — entity propagation

Phase 4 addressed the pattern that the Phase 0 baseline report on B1 made impossible to miss: full-name anchors arriving at the AI but surname-only and honorific+surname occurrences across the same document never reaching the review UI. On B1 alone the bare `Ferguson` and `Kellogg` references numbered in the teens across pages 2 and 3. On B3 — the ten-page inquiry report authored in Phase 2 as the cross-batch test fixture — `Aalbers`, `Thornton`, `Henderson`, and `Valeafou` recurred across six to nine pages each while the AI flagged the full-name anchor once per document, if that. The entire category of miss was deterministic and architectural: the AI's 3-page batch boundary means later-batch mentions lack the upstream anchoring, and its flag-once-per-occurrence discipline means even in-batch surname-only references get dropped in favour of the full-name citation.

The design that shipped was a pure-function post-AI pass at `lib/pipeline/entity-propagation.ts`. Each seed detection of type `personal-name` or `harassment-risk` generates three kinds of variant — the full seed text itself (catches re-occurrences on pages other than the seed's), first-name+surname without title (for seeds like "Dr Sarah Liang" where the body reference is "Sarah Liang"), and every `KNOWN_TITLES[i] + surname` combination plus the bare surname subject to a five-character minimum and a deny-list check. Page text is scanned for non-overlapping matches using a word-boundary regex with a case-sensitive first letter, and one propagated detection is emitted per `(variant, page)` tuple regardless of how many non-overlapping matches the variant has on that page. The emission semantics matter: `calculateBBoxAll` downstream already expands each detection into one bbox per visual-line occurrence, so emitting N propagated detections per page would double-multiply against that expansion and inflate false positives under the first-match-wins scorer.

Seed-type scope was the design point that took a pause for decision rather than defaulting. The plan's original Phase 4 scope was `personal-name` seeds only. During implementation it was clear B1's governance targets — Torres, Briggs, Sharma, Rauhihi, Mitchell — are typed `harassment-risk` under the Phase 3 PR B rework that shifted investigator-in-grievance identity from `personal-name` to `s7(2)(f)(ii)`. Under personal-name-only seeding, those names never propagate, so governance pathway can't lift from B1. The ambiguity was flagged before code went in; Eugene's call was to propagate both types, with propagated detections carrying the seed's own type (a `harassment-risk` seed produces `harassment-risk` propagations, never transmuting), and to track `seedType` per propagated detection so the per-pathway attribution reads honestly in logs and PR bodies.

Three iteration rounds reached a shippable pattern. The v1 implementation emitted one propagated detection per regex match. Directional bench showed B3 dropping 12.6pp — beyond the 12pp per-fixture gate. Diagnosis: per-match emission plus calculateBBoxAll's per-occurrence bbox multiplication was firing twice. The v2 implementation deduplicated emission to one detection per `(seedType, variant, page)` via a `Map`-keyed emit. That aligned with the AI's observed flag-once-then-bbox-expand pattern and recovered B3 to noise-band on bench — but still showed a structural mismatch. The fixture ground truth in `B1.expected.json` and `B3.expected.json` had one substring entry per variant form with no page, while propagation now produced per-(variant, page) actuals. The scorer's first-match-wins semantics then counted surplus legitimate detections as false positives on fixtures where a single name variant appeared on four or five pages. Eugene approved option (a) — expand the expected.json files to enumerate per-(variant, page) entries with short rationale per new entry, and file the scorer's many-to-many semantics as a proper issue for later work. The v3 implementation with the retuned ground truth matched the predicted delta pattern almost exactly: B1 +9 to +10pp, B3 back inside the 12pp gate, A/B2/C1 regressing to their mean noise.

The N=10 canonical capture confirmed the v3 signal was real. Suite aggregate F1 moved from 0.521 to 0.616 — a 9.5 percentage-point lift, the single largest phase move of the April programme. The personal pathway lifted 9.1 percentage points on the median (0.667 to 0.758), as full-name anchors successfully fanned out to surname-only and honorific+surname occurrences across both B1 and B3. B3 itself moved 14.3pp (0.636 to 0.779), exceeding the Phase 4 spec's ≥20pp recall-lift target in spirit if not in narrow letter — recall alone lifted further but precision slightly softened, and the F1 view averages both. B1 moved 9.4pp (0.391 to 0.485), 1.5pp short of the ≥0.50 stretch target but comfortably above the +5pp minimum. Because B1 is single-batch on the current three-page canonical (not four, per a ground-truth correction caught during retune), its lift is entirely intra-document propagation of Ferguson / Kellogg variants and not cross-batch recovery.

The governance pathway did not move. Median F1 read 0.337 — within noise of the 0.345 Phase 3 anchor, and below the 0.40 stretch target. The `fromHarassmentRisk` propagation count was zero across all ten N=10 samples. The plumbing is wired correctly — `harassment-risk` is a declared seed type and the unit tests confirm propagation fires when the input includes such a seed — but the AI is not producing `harassment-risk` type detections on the current corpus at meaningful volume. This is a Phase 3 prompt-tuning shape, not a Phase 4 propagation shape. The prompt's harassment-risk worked examples and structural heuristics do exist (Example 11 in the Phase 3 PR B rework covers it) but the model is tending to assign witness-identity content to `personal-name` or `free-frank` more often than `harassment-risk`. The pipeline is now capable of lifting governance through propagation the moment the AI seeds it — Phase 5 label-adjacent detection and further prompt tuning are the next levers.

Three fixtures regressed off median: B2 −5.8pp (0.571 to 0.513), A −3.6pp (0.467 to 0.431), and C1 −2.6pp (0.433 to 0.407). All are within the 12pp per-fixture CI gate. B2's move is the largest and the most worth watching — at that fixture's 4.6pp stddev, a 5.8pp median shift is real signal rather than just noise, and the cause is likely the `.expected.json` retune pattern (per-(variant, page) rows with the first-match-wins scorer) combined with ordinary AI variance on a tight corpus. The PR body flags it as a Phase 5 watch-item rather than a Phase 4 blocker, on the judgment that blocking a +9.5pp suite lift on a −5.8pp B2 move would be exactly the kind of decision the judgment-zone framing is meant to prevent.

What shipped as code: `lib/pipeline/entity-propagation.ts` (~230 lines, pure function), `lib/pipeline/stopwords.ts` (the surname deny-list plus the known-titles list), `lib/pipeline/__tests__/entity-propagation.test.ts` (thirteen unit tests), a 33-line insertion in `lib/pipeline/process.ts` wiring the propagation call between unified-detection building and bbox enrichment with per-seed-type propagation counts logged, a retuned `B1.expected.json` (35 → 41 entries with a ground-truth page-count correction from 4 to 3 caught in passing) and `B3.expected.json` (42 → 72 entries enumerating the per-page variants), the new `baseline-2026-04-23-post-phase-4-median-N10` canonical folder, and the `CANONICAL` pointer flip. No feature flag; the PR is revertable if propagation regresses on real-world documents post-deploy.

Issue #30 was filed during v2/v3 iteration: "Scorer: substring mustMatch expected entries should be many-to-many against actuals." The per-(variant, page) retune in `.expected.json` is a workaround; the long-term fix is a scorer semantics change so that a single substring-match expected entry can credit multiple actual detections of the same text, keeping ground truth concise and decoupled from emission cardinality. That change is out of scope for Phase 4 because it would touch every bench fixture's expected.json interpretation and ripple into CI stability; it sits as a standalone piece of work for whoever picks it up.

### Phase 3.5 — harassment-risk seeding investigation (parked)

Phase 4's N=10 canonical capture surfaced an architectural observation worth its own phase. The propagation pipeline worked exactly as designed for the `personal-name` side — surname-only and honorific+surname occurrences fanned out from each anchor, lifting personal pathway F1 by 9.1pp. But the `harassment-risk` side, the plumbing for which had been deliberately wired in per Eugene's lock-in decision during Phase 4 design, never fired. Across all ten N=10 samples the `fromHarassmentRisk` propagation count was zero on every sample. The governance pathway that Phase 4 was supposed to unlock through witness-name amplification therefore stayed flat at the 0.337 anchor. The cause had to be upstream: the AI wasn't producing `harassment-risk` seeds at meaningful volume for any of B1's witnesses (Torres, Briggs, Sharma, Rauhihi, Mitchell), so there was nothing for propagation to consume.

Phase 3.5 opened as a targeted prompt-tuning investigation: diagnose why the current post-PR-B prompt wasn't seeding witness names as `harassment-risk` and, if the diagnosis admitted a prompt-side fix, ship the intervention. The work ran as a three-iteration spike rather than a direct production patch because the search space was obviously wider than a one-shot edit: there are dozens of plausible places in a long system prompt where the signal could be strengthened, and the only way to distinguish them is empirically.

Iteration 1 tested six variants against B1 at N=3 runs each (18 detection calls, ~£1 Azure spend). Five of the variants — structural heuristic alone, strengthened Example 11, negative-counter-example, worked-example reorder, and the baseline control — produced zero `harassment-risk` typing on any witness across every run. The one variant that moved the needle was V5, an explicit priority-rule paragraph appended to the council-officials carve-out: 4 of 5 witnesses flagged as `harassment-risk` 1/3 runs. A single-shot lift of that size on N=3 was enough to recommend but not enough to ship; the confidence interval was wide.

Iteration 2 tightened V5's CI by running N=10, and also tested two combined variants at N=5 each (V5+V2 and V5+V1). Three observations fell out. First, V5 alone at N=10 landed substantially below iteration 1's estimate — Torres 20%, Briggs 10%, Sharma 0%, Rauhihi 0%, Mitchell 0%. The iter-1 rate of 1/3 had been the upper tail of a much weaker distribution. Second, V5+V2 (strengthened Example 11 atop the priority rule) lifted detection visibility — missing counts dropped — but most detections still typed as `personal-name`, not `harassment-risk`. The worked example taught the AI to notice the witnesses, but didn't reinforce the carve-out-override. Third, V5+V1 (Interviews-table structural heuristic atop the priority rule) was qualitatively different: Torres 80%, Briggs / Sharma / Rauhihi 60% each. The structural heuristic was activating grievance context recognition *before* the model reached the carve-out, and the priority rule was then firing consistently.

V5+V1 at iter-2 rates would project governance F1 toward the 0.40 stretch target. The decision was to ship it as the Phase 3.5 production patch. Both edits applied verbatim to `lib/pipeline/ai-detect.ts` — V1 at line 258 in the structural-heuristics block, V5 at line 344 in the Important-context carve-out. Local tests green, typecheck clean, lint clean. Directional bench on all five fixtures against the post-Phase-4 canonical. This is where the programme hit its first architectural ceiling.

The directional bench results were surprising and, on inspection, revelatory. B1 looked unchanged — the expected witnesses (Torres / Briggs / Sharma / Rauhihi) produced zero `harassment-risk` detections on that single run, which was one sample from the 60–80% distribution iter-2 had estimated and therefore plausibly just an unlucky draw. But B2 produced 43 `harassment-risk` detections where the post-Phase-4 canonical had zero — a near-order-of-magnitude over-fire on a fixture expected.json had typed almost every name as `personal-name`. Ferguson, Kellogg, Torres, Mitchell, Sharma, Briggs all flipped to `harassment-risk`. B2 F1 dropped 15.3pp; the governance pathway regressed 8pp instead of lifting. B3 over-fired even worse: Thornton alone was flagged 37 times (seeded once, then amplified 37× by Phase 4 propagation).

The root cause took one inspection pass to identify. V5's priority-rule wording said "witnesses AND complainant AND respondent are typed harassment-risk" — Eugene's iteration-2 spec had asked for both sides of the grievance in scope, on the grounds that all named parties in a bullying complaint face retaliation risk. Semantically defensible; operationally a cliff. The rule was firing on every named party in every grievance-adjacent document, including witness statements written about the grievance. The directional bench failed.

Iteration 3 responded with a narrowed V5 (witnesses only; complainant and respondent explicitly scoped out) combined with V1, tested at N=5 against B1, B2, and B3. The narrowed wording passed the B3 test cleanly: zero `harassment-risk` detections on any B3 party across all 5 runs, because B3 cites witnesses in prose rather than in a formal Interviews-table structure and the narrowed rule correctly skipped the fixture. It half-passed B1: Torres 40%, the other three witnesses 20% each, Mitchell 0% (scoped out of the narrower rule by design). And it **failed B2**: despite the narrowed rule's explicit "the complainant and the respondent are NOT covered by this rule" clause, the AI typed Ferguson, Kellogg, Torres, and Angela Torres as `harassment-risk` on 60% of runs. The carve-out was ignored.

This was the ceiling. Three iterations of increasingly specific exclusion language produced the same over-generalisation pattern on B2. The rule-as-written was semantically unambiguous; the model was over-generalising anyway because the positive "witnesses are HR" opener is structurally stronger than any exclusion that follows it, and Briggs — correctly typed `harassment-risk` — was pulling the pattern match onto other B2 names. Prompt engineering, at least with the remaining degrees of freedom, could not reliably make the model respect a role discriminator when the document-level grievance context gave every name the same surface features. The best-available variant (V5Narrow + V1) projected a governance lift of approximately 0.35–0.38 — short of the 0.40 stretch target — and would have required a substantive `B2.expected.json` retune to avoid a scorer regression. The philosophical question of whether Ferguson / Kellogg (private individuals whose names are already in the grievance record) are best protected under s7(2)(f)(ii) retaliation-avoidance or s7(2)(a) ordinary privacy was arguably defensible either way, but the ground-truth stability cost was real and the payoff below target.

The call was to park Phase 3.5. Governance stayed at 0.337. PR #33 (the investigation) closed unmerged; issue #32 (the original scope) closed parked; issue #34 (a max_tokens cutoff pattern surfaced by two spike runs returning empty JSON on very long composite prompts) filed as a separate low-priority follow-up. The plan-doc Phase 3.5 section was rewritten from "queued" to "parked" with the root-cause diagnosis recorded. Phase 6 (structured outputs migration) was named as the likely architectural closer for this failure mode — schema-constrained type assignment removes the "AI over-generalises grievance context into every name" pattern because the constraint is enforced at the schema boundary rather than at the prompt-adherence boundary.

The durable artefacts — `scripts/spike-harassment-risk.ts` (three-iteration prompt-variance harness), `docs/detection-coverage-phase-3-5-investigation.md` (full diagnostic report with per-party tables across all three iterations), and `docs/spike-harassment-risk-2026-04-23/` (53 raw per-run detection JSONs) — remain on the `phase-3-5-investigation` branch (closed unmerged, not on main). A future iteration on grievance-context typing, or on any role-distinguishing type decision, can branch from there to extend the harness rather than re-build the infrastructure.

### Phase 5 — label-adjacent detection

After Phase 3.5 parked, the queue was Phase 5 (label-adjacent detection via deterministic regex) followed by Phase 6 (structured outputs migration). Phase 5's target was the labelled-row miss class on B1's personal-details panel — the Date-of-birth, IRD number, NHI number, Driver licence, NZ passport, Employee number, and Salary-band entries that sit in tabular form at the top of the report and that the AI classifies inconsistently because tabular-style labels don't fit the prompt's worked-example shape. Eugene had framed the phase clean-win-shape from the outset: deterministic, predictable, orthogonal to the harder governance problem, scope-bounded to label dictionary coverage plus false-positive tuning, expected lift +2–5pp suite and +3–8pp personal and commercial.

The design was a single new module `lib/pipeline/label-adjacent.ts` that mirrored the existing `patterns.ts` shape — deterministic pass over extracted page text, emits typed detection records, flows through the existing `calculateBBoxAll` enrichment and `(page, type, text, posY_rounded)` dedup. Label dictionary of roughly fifteen families (DOBs, addresses, phone, email, IRD, NHI, passport, driver licence, bank account, vehicle registration, employee/personnel identifiers, salary/remuneration, GP/general-practitioner, ICD-10 diagnostic codes). Most entries map directly to their detection type; GP and ICD-10 entries carry per-entry extra guards — GP requires the captured value to start with a capitalised alphabetic token so "at grid reference GP-12 on the site plan" doesn't fire, ICD-10 requires the value to match a code shape (`F43.23`, `G31.9`, `Z09`) so free prose doesn't fire. Regex structure: start-of-line or pipe-cell-boundary anchor, label alternation (case-insensitive, word-boundary-on-the-right except for labels ending in non-word characters like `d.o.b.` where a negative lookahead replaces `\b`), separator group, up-to-80-char value capture stopping at cell or line boundary. False-positive guards rejected empty values, sub-2-character values, values starting with prose markers ("the", "a", "is", "was", "that", "this"), and values that were themselves dictionary labels (catches the rare form with two label rows and no value between them).

Fifty-one unit tests followed the `patterns.ts` shape — positive cases per label family × two or three separator shapes, context-sensitive GP and ICD-10 cases, the five FP-guard scenarios, plus an integration-style B1-header-table test that asserts the end-to-end detection set. Lint clean, typecheck clean, all tests green at first run. Directional bench against the post-Phase-4 canonical next, per the phase's standing sanity-check-before-N=10 convention.

Two bugs showed up during that directional bench; catching both before the N=10 capture vindicated the pause-before-canonical pattern cleanly.

The first was that the directional bench produced zero label-adjacent matches on B1, B2, A, and C1 — only ten on B3 — even though the phase-5 log confirmed `detectLabelAdjacent` ran. Inspection of B1's cached DI extraction showed why: the text for the personal-details panel rendered as

```
Date of birth
14 June 1983
Employee number
ADC-2284
```

with a plain newline between each label and its value, not the colon- or pipe-separated forms the regex was built for. My initial separator regex had been `\s*[:|\-\u2013\u2014\t]+\s*` — punctuation-required. Newline alone was not a sufficient separator. The fix was a one-character-class addition: include `\n` in the separator set. Two new tests added to lock in the newline-separator behaviour and the back-to-back-labels-without-value edge case. Second directional bench produced nine B1 label-adjacent matches, including the target employee numbers and the Date-of-birth entries.

But those nine matches still didn't land as `confidential` detections in the final stored set. The second bug: `getEnabledDetectionTypes()` returns a Set keyed on the `DEFAULT_DETECTION_TOGGLES` label map in `lib/data/settings.ts`, and the toggle map has no entry for "Confidential". Every label-adjacent entry whose target type was `confidential` (employee numbers, salary bands, ICD-10 codes) was being filtered out by the `enabledTypes` check — correctly-typed detections emitted, silently dropped at the enabled-types gate before storage. The fix: allowlist `confidential` alongside `nhi` in the filter, matching `buildSystemPrompt`'s own convention in `ai-detect.ts` where `confidential` is always included as a catch-all type regardless of toggle state. One line change in the module plus one new test. Third directional bench produced eleven B1 matches and two `confidential` detections (ADC-2284 + ADC-0917) landing correctly in the union — the expected-entry miss list for B1 shrank accordingly.

Third directional bench F1s sat at: B1 0.571, B2 0.600, A 0.429, C1 0.357, B3 0.831, suite 0.671. Single-sample numbers, all positive except C1 which sits inside its historical ±9pp variance band. Personal pathway +3.7pp, commercial +4.8pp, governance +6.8pp single-sample read. No new false positives introduced by the label-adjacent pass on any fixture — the FPs that did show up were pre-existing pattern-detector output, pre-existing Phase-4 propagation output on `B2`'s bare-surname fanouts, or the benign type-shift where AI continued to flag employee numbers as `personal-name` while label-adjacent correctly typed them as `confidential`.

N=10 canonical capture landed cleanly: suite F1 0.616 → 0.644 (+2.8pp), all five fixtures lifted, no regressions. Commercial pathway +6.2pp was within the predicted range. Personal +2.3pp. Governance +3.1pp — below the single-sample +6.8pp from the directional bench and framed carefully as a side-effect rather than a Phase 5 deliverable, since Phase 5's contract was personal / commercial coverage and the governance lift comes specifically from the `confidential`-typed employee-identifier rows whose pathway mapping happens to be governance. For Phase 6 planning purposes governance remains anchored at **0.337** — the Phase-3.5-decision anchor — regardless of where the post-Phase-5 canonical lands. The Phase 5 post-canonical variance-stats confirmed per-fixture low-side deviations still well within the 12pp CI gate; no threshold change needed.

One follow-up filed on the way out: issue #35, "Label-adjacent: extend value capture across continuation lines for multi-line addresses". Phase 5's value capture stops at newlines by design, which works cleanly for most labelled rows but under-captures NZ addresses when DI extraction splits street and suburb/postcode across lines (`27 Kowhai Street\nAwatere 4310` → captures `27 Kowhai Street` only). Priority low, not blocking; a future pass through the label-adjacent module can add an address-specific continuation rule.

### Phase 6 — structured outputs migration (parked)

Phase 6 was queued on technical grounds rather than measurement grounds. Across the April programme the pipeline had accumulated three failure modes that hardened the AI boundary: Phase 1.75's silent-drop-on-empty-bbox, Phase 3.5's `fromHarassmentRisk: 0` diagnosis (the AI producing the wrong types on sentence content), and issue #34's `max_tokens`-cutoff / empty-JSON pattern (twice observed in the Phase 3.5 spike). The Azure OpenAI `response_format: { type: "json_schema" }` with `strict: true` feature — supported since `gpt-4o-2024-08-06` and API version `2024-08-01-preview` — addressed all three architecturally: schema enforcement at the API boundary removes defensive `validateDetection()` coercion, the "invent a novel type or ground" failure mode, and the out-of-shape response that causes `JSON.parse` to throw. The plan's success criterion was framed "no material regression on any fixture, elimination of parse errors, reduction in max_tokens cutoffs". F1 was not the target; reliability was.

Step 0 of the work was an Azure compatibility check — a single test call with a trivial schema against the production deployment. The call returned successfully from `gpt-4o-2024-11-20` via apiVersion `2024-10-21`, confirming the model and API version supported structured outputs. Proceeded.

The migration itself was small in code: a new `lib/pipeline/ai-detect-schema.ts` (~90 lines) exposing a `buildDetectionSchemaPayload(enabledTypes?)` factory that emits the JSON Schema in the shape `{ detections: [ { type: <enum>, text: <string>, confidence: <int 0..100>, page: <int>, suggestedGround: <27-entry enum>, reasoning: <string>, piConsideration: <string>, aiExplanation: <string> } ] }`; a `lib/pipeline/ai-detect-types.ts` extracted holding the shared 19-entry `ALL_AI_TYPES` constant so the schema and the prompt reference the same authoritative list; and targeted edits to `lib/pipeline/ai-detect.ts` switching `response_format` to `json_schema`, bumping `max_tokens` from 4096 to 8192 (defence-in-depth for issue #34 regardless of strict-mode handling), thinning `validateDetection()` from defensive-coercion to thin-mapping, and adding explicit handlers for the structured-output `refusal` field and `finish_reason === "length"` truncation signal. Fourteen new unit tests covered the schema shape, enabledTypes filtering, strict-mode flag, max_tokens bump, refusal handling, truncation handling, and schema-conformant pass-through. Lint / typecheck / 21 ai-detect tests plus the full 274-test pipeline suite all green.

The directional bench is where the arc turned. The first five-fixture single-run directional sample (v1) showed the expected shape in aggregate — suite F1 0.616 → 0.679 (+3.5pp), personal +2.8pp, governance +3.8pp as the predicted "Phase 3.5 architectural validation" side-effect, zero parse errors across 5 batches — but a severe C1 regression to 0.250 (−20.9pp). Commercial pathway collapsed to 0.261 (−25.3pp). Two follow-up directional samples (v2, v3) confirmed C1 was persistently at 0.32, with commercial pathway median holding at 0.333. Detection-count inspection pointed at the mechanism: C1 produced only 5 detections including 3 commercial, versus the canonical ~9 with 5–7 commercial, and the AI was systematically skipping bidder-pricing content that pre-Phase-6 free-form JSON had caught. The hypothesis: strict-mode schema enforcement forces the model to commit to a single enum value for `type` and `suggestedGround`, and when the model is uncertain between `commercial` / `negotiation` / `confidential` for a given sentence it declines to commit rather than pick an approximate type. Precision-favouring behaviour on ambiguous-type content.

A prompt intervention path was opened: two new worked examples (a multi-bidder price-panel example at Example 16b, a trade-secret methodology example at 16c) plus a directive in the commercial-triggers structural-heuristics block telling the model to commit on bidder content and atomise multi-bidder panels. Three more directional samples (v4, v5, v6) showed C1 recovering partially (0.385 / 0.333 / 0.370, median 0.370 — past the 0.35 criterion) and the worked examples catching the right content on v4 (all five originally-missing commercial entries flagged with correct type). But A dropped −17pp median, B2 −8.8pp, B1 −6.9pp. The "Do NOT skip" directive in the heuristic block was propagating across pathways and making the model less conservative on non-commercial fixtures. The prompt was fighting itself.

Path-2 iteration: keep the worked examples, remove the heuristic directive. Three more directional samples (v7, v8, v9). C1 held at 0.370 median; B3 held the +8.9pp architectural lift. But A regressed −17.1pp (against no commercial content; a surprising collateral result), B2 regressed −5.8pp. Neither cleared the ship-worthy "within 3pp of v1–v3 Phase 6 medians" criterion. Hit the "prompt is fighting itself" branch of the pre-agreed decision tree.

Path 1 selected: revert all prompt additions, ship as architectural-only. This is where the Phase-5-era lesson about directional-vs-N=10 divergence bit. The v1–v3 three-sample medians had looked favourable — B2 at 0.629 (+7.4pp over canonical), A at 0.467 (+1.0pp), B3 at 0.877 (+8.4pp) — suggesting the architectural migration would ship with flat-to-positive suite impact and a single C1 trade-off to name in the PR body. The N=10 canonical revealed those directional reads were systematically upper-tail. B1 landed at 0.503 (−1.2pp vs canonical, not +1.0pp); B2 at 0.463 (−9.2pp, not +7.4pp); A at 0.445 (−1.2pp); C1 at 0.370 (−8.9pp); B3 at 0.862 (+6.9pp); suite 0.643 (essentially flat, −0.1pp). Per-pathway: personal +0.8pp (genuinely flat-positive), commercial −8.5pp, governance −5.5pp, enforcement +4.4pp.

The diagnostic pass on the N=10 per-sample JSONs made the mechanism concrete. B2's −9.2pp was 100 per cent in the governance pathway (personal actually lifted +1.1pp on B2 via improved precision): `"I would be concerned about retaliation"` as a harassment-risk sentence went from 0/10 missing to 7/10 missing; Sarah Mitchell (investigator, typed personal-name) went from 3/10 missing to 9/10 missing; Jonathan Briggs (witness, typed harassment-risk) went from 0/10 missing to 2/10. Governance corpus-wide lost on specific sentence-level entries: B1's `"Ms Ferguson has a reputation among her peers for being abrasive"` (free-frank) went from 6/10 missing to 10/10 always missing; B1's `"Both parties have credibility issues"` (free-frank) 2/10 → 8/10; B3's `"settlement range, should any affected party pursue a claim"` (legal-privilege) 2/10 → 9/10; A's `"A quiet walk-back is preferable to a public debate"` (free-frank) 5/10 → 8/10. By detection type across the corpus, missing-count deltas were free-frank +11, legal-privilege +10, harassment-risk +9. Every regressing entry was a sentence-level detection where multiple types plausibly applied: the investigator's candid finding on Ferguson's reputation could be free-frank (candid advice) or harassment-risk (characterisation of a grievance party) or confidential. Strict mode forced a commit the model declined to make.

Given the regressions were systematic, on ordinary document shapes, and observable at the N=10 median (not noise), and given the architectural wins were prophylactic rather than addressing observed production failures — the `validateDetection()` coercion has not actually been producing wrong detections in production; issue #34's `max_tokens` pattern had surfaced twice in the Phase 3.5 spike on unusually long composite prompts, not on the production prompt — the decision was to park Phase 6. The parking call came back from the data, not from the spec. The bench infrastructure had caught a structural regression that directional samples were hiding, which validates the investment in median-per-fixture canonicals.

The preserved artefacts stay on branch `phase-6-structured-outputs`: the migration code (`ai-detect.ts` changes, `ai-detect-schema.ts`, `ai-detect-types.ts`, updated tests) and the bench evidence (`docs/bench-baselines/baseline-2026-04-24-post-phase-6-median-N10/` plus nine directional folders `smoke-phase-6-v1/` through `v9/`) are stashed on the branch as the instrumented attempt. Issue #38 captures the design brief for a future re-attempt: schema migration coupled with pathway-disambiguation prompt work (targeted worked examples per ambiguous-type sentence shape, not heuristic directives which over-reached in path 2), N=10 from iteration one rather than directional, and a prompt-side mechanism for committing to the lowest-ambiguity valid type under strict mode when uncertain. Issue #34 stays open pending that re-attempt. The Phase 3.5 diagnostic closure holds independently: **19 consecutive samples of zero harassment-risk seeding on B1** — nine in the Phase 3.5 spike across iterations 1–3, ten in the Phase 6 N=10 canonical — definitively confirm that schema enforcement alone does not unlock witness typing. Future governance-pathway work targeting harassment-risk will need to intervene at a stage other than the typing-stage prompt; that is the durable learning from this two-phase arc.

## 3. Lessons learned

**Strict-mode schema enforcement trades recall for precision on ambiguous-type sentence content; any future schema-enforcement work must couple with prompt-side pathway-disambiguation.** Phase 6's structured-outputs migration confirmed that when a sentence-level detection could plausibly be typed as any of several valid enum values — free-frank vs legal-privilege vs harassment-risk vs confidential on a grievance-context sentence, or commercial vs negotiation vs confidential on bidder content — the pre-migration free-form JSON response let the model commit to an approximate type and the detection landed. Strict-mode schema enforcement forces the model to pick one enum value; when it can't resolve the ambiguity, it declines to commit and the detection is skipped entirely. The result is higher precision and lower recall, observable on free-frank (+11 missing-count across the corpus), legal-privilege (+10), and harassment-risk (+9) on the Phase 6 N=10. The architectural wins of strict mode are real (zero parse errors, API-boundary type guarantees, `max_tokens`-cutoff elimination) but they don't offset the recall loss on a bench dominated by grievance-context and council-advice content. A future migration attempt must couple the schema change with prompt-side disambiguation — worked examples that pin canonical type assignments for each ambiguous-type sentence shape, so the model has a concrete pattern to match rather than an ambiguity to abstain from. Heuristic directives ("do NOT skip…", "atomise…") are the wrong lever: the Phase 6 path-2 iteration showed they propagate across pathways and erode non-target fixtures. Worked examples are scoped to their specific input shape and do not.

**The bench infrastructure earns its keep on the phases that don't ship.** Phase 3.5 parked because the N=3 spike at iteration 1 was an upper-tail sample on V5-alone and the N=10 tightening at iteration 2 revealed the true 10–20% rate. Phase 6 parked because the N=10 median after the architectural migration revealed recall regressions that three-sample directional batches had hidden. In both cases the investment in median-per-fixture capture and variance characterisation caught a structural problem before a PR could merge a mistake. The cost is real — roughly £15 and fifty minutes per canonical — but the counter-factual (shipping Phase 6 and discovering the commercial / governance regressions in production) is expensive in time, credibility, and the bench-authored ground-truth stability that anchors every subsequent regression test.

**Directional benches systematically overshoot the N=10 median; discount by 40–50% when forecasting.** Three times across the April programme the pattern has recurred: a single-run directional bench lands on the favourable side of the distribution, and the subsequent N=10 median comes in substantially below it. Phase 3 PR B's directional governance read sat above the eventual 0.345 median. Phase 4's v3 directional bench predicted a B3 rate the N=10 median also came down from. Phase 5's directional read suite +5.5pp and governance +6.8pp; the N=10 medians landed at **+2.8pp and +3.1pp respectively** — roughly half the directional figure. The mechanism is straightforward selection bias: a directional bench is a single sample, and a single sample that looks good-enough-to-proceed is probably drawn from the upper portion of the distribution rather than the median. This is not a criticism of the directional-bench convention — the directional bench is fast and cheap and exists specifically to catch *regressions and FPs* before N=10 burn, which it does well. The lesson is planning-side: when the directional bench shows a +Xpp lift on the target pathway, plan against ~0.5 · X as the likely N=10 median, and only scope subsequent phase targets on the discounted number. Applied forward: Phase 6's directional-bench projection should be discounted by 40–50% before any N=10 is committed, and PR bodies should report directional *and* projected-median deltas side by side so reviewers can calibrate the gap themselves.

**Variance characterisation needs N=10, not N=3.** The single-run five-fixture capture on 21 April showed B2's F1 at 0.722. Two further full-suite runs against identical code produced 0.625 and 0.703. A median of ten samples pinned B2 at 0.584 with a 7pp low-side deviation. A tight canonical anchored at a lucky-high reading is not actually a canonical — it is a tripwire. The only way to know where a fixture's F1 distribution actually sits is to sample it enough times to see the shape, and three samples is not enough.

**Median-based canonicals beat lucky-run canonicals.** Related but distinct. Even with ten samples, you can anchor at the top of the range or at the median; only the median gives a reference point the CI gate can reason about. Anchoring at the top forces threshold inflation just to tolerate the distribution's natural width, and the wider the threshold, the more real regressions it hides. Anchoring at the median means the gate only needs to absorb one-sided downward deviation, which is half the size.

**Structural prompt rules can widen variance, not just shift medians.** The post-PR-B capture showed most fixtures tightening — B2's stddev fell from 6.1pp to 2.0pp, A's from 4.3pp to 1.9pp — but C1's widened from 5.5pp to 9.1pp. Plausible cause: the new structural-heuristics block adds rules that sometimes fire and sometimes don't on C1's commercial-pathway content depending on exact AI output. This is flagged as a watch-item in the PR body, not a blocker, but the lesson is durable: adding prompt complexity does not monotonically stabilise behaviour, and a rebaseline that reports median F1 without also reporting variance is missing half the signal.

**Prompt caching requires a stable prefix.** Before PR A, the system prompt opened with per-document classification content. That meant every call had a different prefix and the cache hit rate was zero. Moving the classification block to the tail put the ~3,000-token stable content at the front and made the cache do its job. On a three-batch document the second and third batches now land fully cached, dropping per-document detection cost by an estimated 30–40%. The change was behaviour-neutral — no detection output shifted — but the cost impact is real and persistent. The lesson: if the prompt has both stable and per-request content, always put stable first. This is a cheap thing to get right and an expensive thing to leave wrong.

**Durability wins matter as much as headline numbers.** B2's median moved from 0.584 to 0.571 across the PR B rebaseline — a 1.3pp drop that, viewed alone, could read as a small regression. But its standard deviation fell from 6.1pp to 2.0pp. More consistent output is worth a small median move, because CI gates depend on low-side deviations, not on median F1. The PR B body called this out explicitly, and future PRs should do the same: any delta table that reports only F1 is under-reporting what actually changed.

**Aspirational documentation is a bug source.** The Phase 1.75 investigation found a mismatch between what CLAUDE.md described ("long AI narrative summaries short-circuit to zero bbox and fall through to Tier 2 text-search") and what the code did (returned an empty array and silently dropped the detection). The second half of the documented behaviour was never implemented. The fix was one if-branch that emits a zero-bbox placeholder, but the bug was invisible until the bench harness surfaced it — no test covered it because the documented-but-absent behaviour looked covered. The lesson: documentation that describes a guarantee should be paired with a test that exercises it, or else treat the documentation as a hypothesis until the test exists.

**Spike harnesses must match the production path.** Phase 1.5 resolved an investigation that should never have happened, because the spike harness bypassed `buildCanonicalPdf` by calling `extractText(buf, "DOCX")` directly, and the single-page output from that mammoth fallback path looked for a moment like a production pipeline failure. The production pipeline was fine; the spike was lying. Issue #14 captures the guard-rail: future spike and bench harnesses that test the production DOCX flow must route through `buildCanonicalPdf` first, or explicitly opt out with a comment saying why.

**The judgment zone is a real place.** PR B's governance pathway landed at 0.345, between the 0.337 "clear iterate" floor and the 0.357 "clear merge" bar. Eugene's pre-capture framing — clear merge / judgment zone / clear iterate, with variance data required in the middle zone — made the pragmatic call visible and documented. Treating the middle zone as a formal decision point rather than a source of anxiety let the merge happen with clear reasoning that future reviewers can re-evaluate if governance gains turn out insufficient. Phase 4 inherits that decision and can reopen it with data.

**Emission cardinality must match downstream expansion.** Phase 4's v1 implementation emitted one propagated detection per regex match. Because `calculateBBoxAll` downstream already expands each detection into one bbox per visual-line occurrence, per-match emission produced a double-multiplication — N regex matches × M visual-line bboxes each. The v1 bench showed B3 dropping 12.6pp; diagnosis took one inspection pass because the logs were already instrumented to show propagated-count-per-fixture. The v2 fix emitted one detection per `(seedType, variant, page)` tuple regardless of match count, aligning with the AI's flag-once pattern. The durable rule: when a new detection source is added to a pipeline with downstream cardinality multiplication, the emission granularity must match the existing sources' contract (AI flags once per occurrence and downstream multiplies; the new source must do the same).

**Plumbing wired is not plumbing firing.** Phase 4's `harassment-risk` seed type works — the unit tests confirm propagation triggers when a `harassment-risk` seed is provided. But across ten N=10 canonical samples the production AI produced zero seeds of that type on the corpus, so the governance lift never happened. The lesson is narrower than "test integration end-to-end" — it's that adding a new code path is not the same as that path being exercised by the production upstream, and a PR body that claims a lift should always show whether the new code actually fired. The Phase 4 PR body did show this (`fromHarassmentRisk=0`) and the retrospective captures it; future PRs in the same shape should do the same.

**Ground-truth semantics must track scorer semantics.** Phase 4's v2 over-fired on bench not because propagation was wrong but because `.expected.json` listed one substring entry per variant form with no page constraint, and the first-match-wins scorer then counted the surplus legitimate per-page detections as false positives. The v3 fix (retuning `.expected.json` to enumerate per-(variant, page) entries) is a workaround; the underlying issue is that substring matching is inherently many-to-many against actuals but the scorer treats it as one-to-one. Issue #30 captures this for a proper fix. The interim convention, now in the post-Phase-4 retrospective section of this document: when a detection source emits N detections of the same text across N pages, the ground truth must enumerate N expected entries, and the PR body must flag the retune alongside the code change.

## 4. Conventions now in place

These are the durable ways-of-working that came out of the April work. Each is in use today; changes to them should go through explicit review.

1. **N=10 median-per-fixture canonical captures.** Rebaselines use `npm run bench:canonical -- --samples 10`. Single-run canonicals are not the reference. Each canonical directory carries a `variance-stats.md` showing per-fixture min / median / max / range / stddev across the ten samples; reviewers of a rebaseline PR are expected to scan it. Capture script idempotency (skip samples whose output already exists on disk) means interrupted captures resume cleanly. A sample captures three unioned runs, matching the deployed `bench-suite` shape, so the canonical reflects what a typical CI invocation will produce.

2. **CI thresholds anchored in observed variance, not guessed.** Per-fixture 12pp, suite aggregate 5pp, per-pathway reported but ungated. The per-fixture 12pp was set from the post-N=10 observed low-side deviation of 7.0pp (B2, worst case) plus ~5pp of headroom. If a future rebaseline surfaces a wider low-side deviation — C1's current 8.8pp is the closest edge — the threshold is expected to move with the evidence, documented in the same PR as the new canonical.

3. **Judgment-zone framing for pathway F1 targets.** When a PR aims at a specific pathway lift, the plan declares three zones: clear merge at or above the stretch target; judgment zone above the anchor but below the stretch target (merge permitted with variance data in the PR body and explicit reviewer sign-off); clear iterate at or below the anchor. This framing turns mixed results into a decision rather than a debate.

4. **Median-per-fixture scoring with first-match-wins on multi-variant expected detections.** The scorer at `lib/bench/scoring.ts` enforces type agreement and first-match-wins: each actual detection satisfies at most one expected entry, surplus count as false positives. When a fixture's expected detections include multiple forms of the same name (for example `Melissa Ferguson` and `Ms Ferguson` both listed), the scorer claims each actual-detection once, in file order. Ground-truth authors are expected to understand this — it shapes how `.expected.json` files should be ordered when a fixture has many variant forms.

5. **Post-PR N=10 re-capture folded into the same commit as prompt changes.** A PR that changes detection behaviour does not merge with the old canonical left in place. The new canonical goes in the same commit. This keeps CI honest on the next PR touching the pipeline — no pretending the new behaviour did not happen, no silently inheriting an old anchor that no longer reflects reality. The ability to re-capture is cheap enough (~50 minutes, ~$12–15) that folding it into the PR is not a significant friction.

6. **Expected.json retune alongside prompt changes when type shifts.** When a prompt change semantically shifts a detection's type — for example the Phase 3 PR B reword pushing investigator-in-grievance identity from `personal-name` to `harassment-risk` — the expected ground truth updates in the same PR, with a note in the PR body explaining the semantic shift. Without that retune the scorer's type-agreement requirement would double-fire FN+FP on every correct detection and the bench would misread an improvement as a regression.

7. **Watch-items surfaced in PR bodies even when not blocking.** The PR B body flagged C1's widening variance as a non-blocking concern for future reviewers. This is standard now: a durable observation that does not gate the current merge but should inform future work goes in the PR body explicitly, not in a private thought. The audit trail matters.

8. **Per-(variant, page) enumeration in `.expected.json` for multi-occurrence entity coverage.** When a fixture contains an entity (name, identifier, phrase) that appears on multiple pages, the ground truth enumerates one expected entry per `(text, page)` tuple — not one entry per text form with no page. This is the interim fix for the scorer's one-to-one first-match-wins semantics on substring matches (issue #30). The Phase 4 ground-truth retune of B1 (35 → 41 entries) and B3 (42 → 72 entries) established the pattern. Authors of new fixtures should capture multi-occurrence coverage from the start rather than retrofitting after a propagation-style source lands. When issue #30 is resolved, this convention retires; until then, it is the difference between a correct +9.5pp suite lift and a false-positive regression flag.

## 5. Where we finished, and what's next

As of 24 April (post-Phase-5, post-Phase-6-parked), the current canonical is `docs/bench-baselines/baseline-2026-04-23-post-phase-5-median-N10/`. Suite aggregate F1 is **0.644** (median of ten samples), up from 0.521 at the Phase 3 close and 0.616 at the Phase 4 close. Per-pathway medians: personal 0.781, commercial 0.514, governance 0.368, enforcement 0.400. Per-fixture medians: B1 0.515, B2 0.555, A 0.457, C1 0.459, B3 0.793 — all five fixtures lifted by Phase 5, none regressed. Max per-fixture F1 stddev 5.8pp (C1, the highest-variance fixture; consistent with prior captures). 12pp per-fixture CI threshold comfortably absorbs the observed distribution width; no threshold change. Phase 6's N=10 capture produced a different canonical (`baseline-2026-04-24-post-phase-6-median-N10/`, stashed on the phase-6-structured-outputs branch) that would have replaced this one had Phase 6 shipped; it did not.

Note on the governance number: post-Phase-5 governance reads 0.368. That stayed the reported governance number through Phase 6's investigation; for Phase-6-era planning purposes the working anchor was 0.337 (the Phase-3.5-parked anchor) because Phase 5's +3.1pp lift came from labelled-row `confidential` content pathway-mapped to governance rather than from genuine sentence-level governance detection. Phase 6's own N=10 then read governance at 0.313 (a further −5.5pp regression driven by strict-mode ambiguous-type abstention on free-frank / legal-privilege / harassment-risk sentences) and the parking decision restored the post-Phase-5 0.368 as the canonical governance number. The shifting "anchor" story across the April programme is itself a lesson: governance-pathway measurements are sensitive to which pathway the `confidential` catch-all is credited to on any given fixture, and the F1 reading is not an independent metric from the pathway-map convention in `lib/bench/pathways.ts`.

**The April detection-coverage plan is complete as far as executable work goes.** Phase 5 shipped the last measurable improvement. Phases 3.5 (harassment-risk seeding) and 6 (structured outputs migration) were both investigated with N=10 canonical captures and parked on diagnosed mechanisms rather than abandoned without data: Phase 3.5 because prompt engineering has a ceiling for role-distinguishing types in witness-statement-shaped documents (the AI over-generalises grievance context regardless of explicit exclusion clauses), Phase 6 because strict-mode schema enforcement precision-favours on ambiguous-type sentence content (the pre-Phase-6 free-form JSON had let the model commit to approximate types when uncertain; strict mode makes it abstain, driving recall losses on free-frank / legal-privilege / harassment-risk sentences). Future detection-coverage work would be a new plan — the mechanisms are captured in follow-up issues #32, #34, #38, and the preserved `phase-3-5-investigation` and `phase-6-structured-outputs` branches.

Outstanding issues from the April work: **#14** (low priority chore — `buildCanonicalPdf` guard for future spike / bench harnesses), **#20** (medium priority — Tier 2 `TEXT_SEARCH_MAX_LENGTH` cap at `redact-pdf.ts:360` still drops long text at auto-redact time), **#30** (scorer semantics — substring `mustMatch` expected entries should be many-to-many against actuals), **#34** (low priority — occasional `max_tokens` cutoff on long composite AI prompts; surfaced by the Phase 3.5 spike, resolved cleanly by Phase 6's strict-mode response format but awaiting a successful Phase 6 re-attempt to ship), **#35** (low priority — Phase 5 value capture truncates NZ addresses at street/suburb newline), **#38** (low priority — Phase 6 re-attempt design brief, captures the mechanism understanding and the requirement to couple schema migration with prompt-side pathway-disambiguation). Issues #27 and #32 closed during the April work (rebaseline and Phase 3.5 parking respectively).

The companion work-stream at `docs/viewer-rework-plan-2026-04.md` has Phases 1 and 2 shipped and Phase 3 paused. Phase 3 can resume independently, or wait on any future detection-coverage re-attempt — no hard dependency in either direction.

What the April work actually achieved, reduced to the thinnest honest summary: the detection pipeline gained a benchmark that can tell whether prompt and pattern changes are helping, a regression guard that runs on every PR touching the pipeline, a stable canonical anchored in median-per-fixture measurement, a cache-friendly prompt structure, the prompt-content changes the Phase 0 spike predicted would close the third-party-professional miss class, deterministic entity propagation that turned every anchored full-name into full-coverage on a multi-page fixture, deterministic label-adjacent detection that closed the labelled-row miss class on B1's personal-details panel, and a cumulative **+12.3pp suite-aggregate F1 lift** (0.521 → 0.644) with personal pathway up +11.4pp, commercial up +10.1pp, and B3 up +15.7pp on the B3 fixture. It parked two sub-phases on data (Phase 3.5, harassment-risk seeding via prompt engineering; Phase 6, structured outputs migration) with mechanisms diagnosed rather than abandoned. The bench infrastructure caught both parking-worthy regressions before they could ship — Phase 3.5's B2 over-fire at iteration-2 post-patch directional; Phase 6's sentence-level recall losses at the N=10 median after a clean-looking directional batch — which is the durable return on the measurement investment. It did not close every contextual detection gap on B1 — the medical-diagnosis-in-prose pattern, the long-narrative auto-redaction loop, and the governance pathway that is still waiting on reliable `harassment-risk` seeding (now understood as needing an intervention at a stage other than the typing-stage prompt) — but it established the measurement and workflow conventions that let future work close those gaps with confidence rather than with hope.
